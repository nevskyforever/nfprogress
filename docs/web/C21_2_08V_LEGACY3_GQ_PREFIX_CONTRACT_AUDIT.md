## Entry boundaries and eligible cohorts

G and Q use distinct runtime instances and certificate ownership. The default G entry authenticates a first-generation F/H historical source chain and the complete account inventory. Q is selected explicitly by `bindMixedSources()` when authenticated history indicates an independent non-root source event or later source revision. Q then runs the full mixed-source validator; a Q error is terminal and cannot fall back to G. Neither entry may infer authority from H’s public counts or from one authenticated Note.

For the next implementation, the smallest safe cohort is one genuine F Legacy3 root, all four accepted F strategies, eligible H descendants of its original Note and (for `keep_both`) retained Note, and no independent project/source history. This is G-only. Q should be enabled only after its independent-source, membership, source-revision and event-order cases pass the same full-history acceptance. Original and retained identities stay distinct; one F `keep_both` Resolution is one server sequence and one Resolution family count.

Raw Legacy ordinary received-on-3, a second Resolution generation, own/local unpublished authorship, implicit tombstone resurrection, unsupported mixed ownership, incomplete original publication, missing/cold dependencies, unknown families and open independent forks are not admitted. An authentic unresolved H peer fork may be retained as history, but that incoming sequence remains unqualified and blocks the contiguous prefix.

## Account-wide prefix result and write boundary

The existing G/Q calculator authenticates family evidence for every server sequence from 1 through `pull_cursor`. It preserves `current_proven_prefix`, advances `candidate_proven_prefix` only over a dense qualified prefix, and stops at the earliest gap (`missing_sequence`) or present but unqualified event (`unproven_family`). It does not skip a row to reach a later event. Existing corruption at or below `current_proven_prefix` fails closed rather than lowering an already claimed prefix. If complete-history authentication itself fails, preparation rejects and returns no prefix result; W must not fabricate an earliest blocker from unauthenticated evidence.

Legacy3 G/Q must retain exact original encrypted Resolution identity and F Resolution4/Proof1/inbox10 closure; authenticate all accepted H rows and source history; and prove Metadata/Stage/StageOrder dependencies with their real owners. Unrelated project rows still participate in the account-wide sequence scan. `keep_both` contributes one Resolution sequence, not one sequence per output Note.

The existing certificate pins the scope, owner, device/auth/key/profile lifecycle and complete ten-store inventory. Calculation reloads and compares that inventory. A separately minted commit plan rechecks the full inventory in the native IndexedDB `readwrite` transaction, cursor relationships, lifecycle, calculated result and compare-and-swap state. The only permitted mutation is `cursors.proven_prefix`; outcomes are `advanced`, `already_proven`, or `no_progress`. It must not write source records, inbox rows, receipts, outbox, ACK, capabilities or any production scheduler state. ACK remains independent and false.

This keeps old F2/Content3 behavior and the generic root guard unchanged. The H-only grant cannot authorize G/Q, and a G grant cannot authorize Q. K, O and private Metadata retain strict wrappers and their negative tests. No frozen proof/schema/wire or cryptographic contract change is demonstrated as necessary: use the accepted durable record shapes and existing AEAD/digest checks. The added guard/certificate is ephemeral. No IndexedDB migration, new durable proof version, backend, wire or crypto change is required on current evidence.
